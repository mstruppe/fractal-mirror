## 1 · The problem and the principle

**The problem (Max's words of record, thirty-third session):** *"The bigger my network the more I need to hedge the token load across different providers."* Every child added to the network adds sessions, flights, and corpus work; if all of it defaults to the most capable model on one vendor's pool, cost scales linearly with the network while the budget does not.

**The principle is already doctrine.** C-066 installed the drone tier with a two-headed rationale: *parallel capacity on separate quota pools + model diversity in verification*. Hedging is not a new idea needing ratification — it is the unexercised half of a ratified one. What was missing is the routing layer: a recorded answer to *which work runs on which model, and why*.

**The gate-coverage principle (this document's one contribution):** *the required model quality is set by gate coverage, not by task prestige.* Where the machine gates (`verify.py`, `check_versions.py`, the pack gates, a flight's adversarial verification tier) catch failure mechanically, a weaker model's mistakes cost a retry, never a defect — C-066's "the reviewer trusts the gate, not your claims" carries an economic corollary. Ungated judgment work is where the flagship earns its price; gated volume work runs on the cheapest model that passes.

## 2 · Two axes: client and model

The **client** is the harness — what reads the repo, holds the session, drives the tools (Claude Code, Codex CLI, Gemini CLI). The **model** is what does the thinking, with its strong suits and its price. Some clients pin the model to one vendor (Claude Code, Codex CLI); others are partly or fully model-agnostic (Gemini CLI, Aider, Goose, OpenHands — Aider will drive nearly any model including local ones). A routing decision therefore records *two* facts per task class: which harness holds the session, and which model runs under it. **The Client Library (C-097) is the canonical home of the client axis; this document holds the model axis.** Neither duplicates the other (C-003 — point, don't duplicate).

## 3 · The three-layer hedge (the routing rule — universal by construction)

**The layer order is set by adoption cost, and it is Max's direction of record (thirty-third session): the native layer is the first thing to look at — improve the workflow without compromising quality — and the framework is universal: every FRACTAL instance's custom solutions build on it, never beside it.** "Native" reads per instance: the mother's home ladder is Anthropic's; KNet's is its client vendor's; a child on any harness applies the same rule to its own ladder. The rule is kernel-shaped; the vendor names are instance values.

Ordered by adoption cost — each layer is cheaper to reach than the next and should be exhausted first:

1. **Down the home vendor's ladder first.** Anthropic's own tiers span roughly **10× in price** (Haiku-class → flagship; snapshot in §6). Claude Code's model picker and per-agent overrides inside flights already reach the whole ladder — no new infrastructure, no new adapter, no new identity. Gated flight lanes, mechanical sweeps, and finder/refuter agents are the first candidates.
2. **Cross-provider drones second.** Separate quota pools — the C-066 install, four flights proven (the store stats tool, the first `close.py`, `genesis.py`, `doctor.py` + the INHERITED checks; 2026-08-14/15). The drone contract is model-agnostic by construction: brief → branch → machine gate → review-merge. A drone's vendor is an implementation detail the gate never sees.
3. **Local models third.** Open-weight models (the Llama class) run at **~zero marginal cost** once the hardware exists — capex instead of opex, no quota pressure at all, and capacity that scales *with* the network instead of against it. Hardware-gated: on Apple Silicon, the 8B class runs on ~16 GB unified memory; the 70B class wants ~48 GB+ even quantized — the reachable aptitude tier is machine-dependent. The natural home is bulk mechanical corpus work under full gate coverage.

**The judgment floor:** governance — closes, doctrine, ratification support, conceptualisation — stays on the flagship model of the governing surface. It is the *small* share of tokens (§6) and the one class where no gate substitutes for quality.

### 3.1 · The quality rules (how to down-tier without compromising)

1. **Tier by gate coverage, never by task prestige** (§1's principle — the router's one question is *what catches this work's failures?*).
2. **Cheap to propose, expensive to accept.** Generation and search lanes down-tier first (a weak finder costs recall, which loop-until-dry recovers); acceptance lanes — judges, assemblers, final verifiers — down-tier last or never (a weak judge costs precision, which nothing downstream recovers). Where verification itself is spread across many agents, diversity of lens can substitute for individual strength — but the top of the funnel stays strong.
3. **The judgment floor never down-tiers:** closes, canon writing, doctrine, anything ungated. The floor binds the *model*, not the spend — **effort modulates within the floor** (mechanical sessions at lower effort, doctrine and close days at full depth): the intra-seat hedge. Rationale of record (Max, thirty-third session, on the seat-tier question): the seat is the smallest token share with the highest quality leverage — and under C-133 the origin's judgment is the network's ceiling; it is the last lane in the down-tier queue. **Clarified same session (Max's resolution of the kernel-integrity alarm):** residual drift is expected at any tier — integrity is carried by the *layered maintenance rituals* (gates → scans → four-eyes → field use; every defect on record was caught by the system's own loops and cured in-session or next close), the seat's tier tuning each ring's per-pass yield, never substituting for the rings. The counterpart duty is **communicated care**: adopters take on the rituals with the system (**the care doctrine — ratified C-137, 2026-08-22**; GENESIS §3.4 its ground).
4. **Move one lane at a time, measured.** Every down-tier lands as a recorded change with its Gas Ledger before/after; quality regressions (gate failures, refuted findings, rework) revert it — C-095, remove on observed drag, applied to routing.

### 3.2 · Customs build on the framework (Max's universality clause)

An instance's custom solution — an installable tool, a drone lane, a local model, a scripted pipeline — **declares its place in this framework at specification time**: which layer it occupies, what gate coverage licenses its tier, and how its spend reaches the instance's gas ledger. No parallel cost logic. The mother's first application: the Mining Tool spec (OQ-35, her seat) is written model-agnostic, batch-capable, and local-capable — the framework's terms — before KNet builds.

## 5 · The pitched instance allocation

Recorded as pitched, per instance — each row an offer to its owner, adoption sovereign (the C-113 grammar):

- **The mother:** Claude Code, flagship model — governance is her load and it does not scale with network content volume. Her flights are the layer-1 candidates.
- **PR (the forming child):** **Claude Code as the governing surface** (Max's call, thirty-third session — the reference client for a newborn; an unproven client would be two experiments at once). Content *production* by briefed drones on other providers — the C-066 shape applied in the child: conceptualise on the governing surface, produce under brief, gate before merge. Media-format work (image, short video) will largely involve generation models driven as pipeline tools, not rival session clients. **Standing flag:** social-platform posting is a publishing gate of the C-120 class — it stays Max's own hand unless he ratifies a standing authorization deliberately, the C-064 way; platform API restrictions (Instagram, TikTok) independently constrain automation.
- **KNet (its jurisdiction):** already on Codex sessions (P-008 — its own pool, the hedge working today). The pitched addition: a **local Llama mining lane** for corpus mechanics — the Mining Tool's build seat is KNet's, and gate-covered bulk work is exactly the local class. Enters only by KNet's own `P-…` decision.

**The network-scaling frame:** each child is its own quota pool by construction — KNet already runs on OpenAI's, not Anthropic's. Routed this way, the mother's spend stays roughly flat while the network grows; the failure mode is only ever *defaulting* high-volume work to the flagship out of habit.

## 6 · The measuring instrument — route from data, never from impressions

**The Gas Ledger (C-129) is this document's data source.** Its record already shows where the tokens go (rows of 2026-08-18/20): **flights dominate by orders of magnitude** — Scan #6 at **4.04M** tokens (60 agents), Scan #5 at **1.62M**, a single 3-agent verification workflow at **165k** — while the judgment layer stays small: closes at ≈40–136k floors, orientations ≈74–85k, session working bodies ≈300–550k. The corpus effect is real but secondary (the mother's close ≈316k vs. a newborn's ≈11k — corpus, not ritual; the diet lever ≈1,350 tokens per 1,000 evicted words per session).

**The method:** the offload list is the ledger's top token classes, taken in order — today that list reads *flights first, corpus work second, sessions third, closes last*. A routing decision cites the ledger rows that justify it; a routed class keeps feeding rows so the decision can be revisited on drift. Pricing snapshots are consulted live at decision time and never held authoritative here (the Client Library's no-runtime-API posture applied to economics) — the dated datum of record, 2026-08-20: Anthropic first-party spans ~$1/$5 (Haiku-class) to ~$10/$50 (Fable-class) per MTok, with a 50% batch discount and prompt caching cutting repeated-context input cost — the spine re-reads every session make caching structurally relevant.

## 7 · What this document is not

Not a conduct source (Conversation Settings). Not a client catalog (Client Library — the harness axis). Not a mandate: every row above is an offer; instances adopt by their own decision, and a never-routing instance is fully conformant. Not a pricing authority: vendor prices drift and are consulted live. And not yet doctrine — everything here except the already-ratified inheritances (C-066's rationale, the gate philosophy) awaits Max's ratification and then field-forging, one row at a time.
