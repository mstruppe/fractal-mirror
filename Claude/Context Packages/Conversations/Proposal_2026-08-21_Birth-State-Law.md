## 1 · The observed ground

The principle already lives in the kernel — **five islands, each built when someone happened to ask the birth-state question:** the GENESIS §2 instance parameters (values); the generated instance documents (BOOTSTRAP, adapter, Register, Index, Global/Local, opener — each a child's own from minute one); the C-131 seed tier (user documents born built-but-empty); the per-instance tool configs `fieldnote_roster.json` and `.version-registry` (jurisdiction constants externalized, written by genesis); and the declared tier-0 capability posture ("add a component when you feel its absence, never by anticipation").

Every fracture in the register sits **exactly where the question wasn't asked**: the Rule Overview shipped grown (F1), the opener grammar shipped mother-shaped (F2), the spec pins shipped stale (F8), the owner's name shipped literal (F9), the authority state shipped ambiguous (F10), the doctor shipped configless (F3/F11). The pattern exists; it was never law; coverage is accidental. This proposal makes it law.

## 2 · The law (draft clause, proposed home: GENESIS — the birth law's own document)

**The two-seam doctrine (the law's backbone — Max's distinction of record):** *birth is a pure function; upgrade is a merge.* A birth has no prior state, so it can and must be `(kernel, interview parameters) → newborn` — deterministic, total, with zero origin biography among its inputs; every birth fracture in the register was a hidden impurity in that function. An upgrade can never be that: an existing instance's value *is* its accumulated state, so migration is `(instance state, new kernel, owner dispositions) → upgraded instance`, and the owner-disposition input is irreducible — that residue is not friction but the sovereignty grammar itself (C-113.b, adoption by own decision). The boundary is sharp and one-way: **birth happens exactly once — after the birth commit, everything is migration** (a young instance's migration is thin, never a re-birth; PR's move to beta-0.8 will already be one). The newborn is *detached, never orphaned*: state and authority reset; lineage and care persist — the birth record (`.inherited`, the born-from release + hash) is the baseline every later migration diffs against. The manifest below serves **both seams — one classification, two consumers**: genesis reads a stance to know what to generate, stamp, or copy; the KMP reads the same stance to know what is mechanically adoptable (ship-neutral), what is the instance's own and never overwritten (generate-fresh / seed — its projections regenerate from local source after adoption), and what is replaceable reference (origin-reference). The register's history is this doctrine violated from both sides — genesis *copying* like an upgrade (F1/F9/F10), KNet's beta-0.5 migration *installing origin projections* like a birth (F1); the law puts each seam back on its own side.

> **The Birth-State Law.** No element enters the kernel without a **declared birth-state** — what this element is, says, and permits in a newborn jurisdiction at minute zero. The declaration runs on four axes:
>
> 1. **Value** — every jurisdiction-bound *value* (name, writers, domains, buffer file, anchor authority, **owner**) is a GENESIS §2 parameter, asked at the interview, stamped at the naming, never inherited literally.
> 2. **Artifact** — every shipped *document* carries one of five stances, declared in the manifest and, where bound, in its own banner: **generate-fresh** (instance-bound, written by genesis from a template) · **seed** (structure ships built-but-empty, C-131 discipline) · **ship-neutral** (jurisdiction-free content, byte-exact by design: specs, formats, procedures, licenses) · **origin-reference** (the origin's biography, explicitly labeled as such, never law in the receiving tree) · **omit** (does not ship; forged on felt need). *An artifact whose content projects a particular instance's state can never be ship-neutral.*
> 3. **Authority** — **a newborn's promotion ledger is empty.** Every authorization is a per-jurisdiction grant, made only by that instance's own owner, recorded in that instance's own register; no promotion, standing authorization, or calibration travels — not in a copy (C-091), and not in a birth. Inherited conduct text states its promotions as the origin's history, never as the reader's live grants.
> 4. **Tool-config** — every tool's jurisdiction-bound constants (paths, rosters, scopes, heuristic exemptions, posture expectations) live in a per-instance config the tool reads and genesis writes — the `fieldnote_roster.json` pattern, mandatory. A tool with no config declares, in the manifest, that it has no jurisdiction-bound behavior.

## 3 · The artifact axis's showcase — the stamped Rule Overview seed (Max's design of record)

The F1 cure demonstrates the law's second and first axes together. The mother's Rule Overview gains an explicit jurisdiction line (*"Rule-set of: FRACTAL, the mother instance — in a copy, this page is the origin's biography, never your law"* — the origin-reference stance carried in the artifact itself, surviving even where tooling fails: manual clones, the frozen tags). And genesis stops shipping it: a `rule_overview()` template joins the generate-fresh family, its indexical stamped at the naming step — *"Rule-set of: {name} — born {date} from the FRACTAL {release} kernel; this page projects {name}'s own effective law and nothing else"* — its body the newborn's whole true law (the adapter's rules, the verify gate, tier-0 posture, the fieldnote regime, P-numbering, the inheritance clause from the child's seat), growing as `P-…` decisions accumulate. **The load-bearing joint: the stamp lands on a seed body, never on the grown body** — stamp answers *whose*, seed body answers *what*; F1 was both wrong at once. Implementation: inline template beside its siblings (Global/Local/Index/Register), so C-131's Discipline-B boundary (the seed tier bounded to the kernel trio) stays untouched; the child edition follows the child's naming law (`{Name}_Rule_Overview.md`), routed by its generated Index.

## 4 · The machine half — the birth-state manifest and its gate

Genesis's keep/generate/omit rosters unify into one **declared birth-state manifest**: every kernel element, its axis entries, its stance, its pinned edition where versioned. Genesis instantiates *from* the manifest; `.inherited` becomes its receipt. The pack gate (`close.py --pack`) gains one check: **the manifest is complete and current** — no unclassified artifact (would have caught F1: "`Fractal_Rule_Overview.md`: unclassified — ship-neutral or instance-bound?"), no stale pin (would have caught F8 at the source: the keep-list naming v0.6 while the tree's newest is v0.7), no parameterless owner-indexical (F9), no configless jurisdiction-bound tool (F11). One check, four fracture classes unrepeatable.

**The manifest is executable — the seed artifact (Max's two-mirror idea, weighed into its one-mirror form):** at every tagging close the pack does not merely check the manifest, it *runs* it — emitting a self-contained **seed artifact**, `FRACTAL-seed-beta-X.Y`, containing exactly the classified kernel a newborn receives: `genesis.py` with its generate-fresh templates, the seed editions, the ship-neutral standards at their manifest-pinned newest, the license layer (C-099 — terms travel), and the `.inherited` provenance record naming the release and hash it derives from. It lives on the **one mirror, one anchor chain, one address** — the **birth door** beside the **update door** (the tag chain + the §8 advisories), served through the site's Downloads surface. What this *deletes*: the runtime keep-list (F8's home ceases to exist — the manifest replaces it, checked once at pack) and genesis's whole subtraction phase (genesis becomes pure instantiation — the §2 function made visible); in the seed, mother-biography leaks are structurally impossible, because unclassified content is simply not present. The existing scratch-birth test becomes the seed's acceptance test. **A second mirror was weighed and declined** on Max's ask (this session's reasoning): a separate birth repo would double the C-116 rider, fork or forgo the C-090 anchor chain, and stand as a third public copy able to drift — against the one-canon principle (the estate plan's competence-and-copies-never-canon; the same session found even two in-repo copies drifted). The two-repo form stays **banked**, not rejected: it becomes natural if the umbrella wakes (OQ-34) or federation (arc 3) matures into a kernel registry serving many seeds.
