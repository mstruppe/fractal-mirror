The Fieldnote Buffer, in one page

Your project’s temporary memory: nothing that pops up gets lost, and nothing stays parked forever. The buffer is the shared organ of the project’s two navigation spines — the one document both you and the agent write.

What it is

A fieldnote is RAM. You are mid-task and something surfaces — a friction, a win, an idea, a question that belongs to another day. It goes into one buffer (Site/Fieldnotes.md), deliberately temporarily. The buffer is a growing list that is meant to shrink: each entry is worked off soon by dissolving into the project — it becomes a rule, a document update, a concept, a transfer — and then it leaves. What remains is the citation in whatever absorbed it, plus the full git history. Nothing lost, nothing hoarded.

The one command — the off-topic jump

/fieldnote <what happened, in your own words>

A normal chat message, never an interrupt — the agent finishes what it’s doing. The command itself carries the meaning: this is deliberately off-topic; capture it, don’t re-aim the work. No target word, no routing decision, no thinking about where it belongs — that question is answered later, at work-down, where it belongs.

What happens next (from your seat)

  1. You say it — one line or ten, your words.
  2. You hear it back — the agent extracts the structured information and reads back what will land: your raw words, its derived reading, and a first category guess (non-binding; “fits nowhere yet” is fine).
  3. You say yes — your ratification is the write gate. Only then is the entry written: your raw words verbatim in a machine block, the ratified reading beneath it. One line of confirmation, and the session returns to exactly where the work stood.

Working it off

At a natural pause — or when the gauge says so — buffer items are worked down. Solving is your governance act: an entry graduates into the project (a register row, a reissue, its own project) or is knowingly kept waiting on a named trigger. A solved entry is deleted whole; its id is never reused.

The pressure gauge

YELLOW at more than 10 entries — surfaced at capture, at session open, and at the close walk. It is a warning, never a gate: a full buffer still captures, always. The threshold is provisional and calibrates by doing — if it ever fires wrong, that observation is itself a fieldnote.
