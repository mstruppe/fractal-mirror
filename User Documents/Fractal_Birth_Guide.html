Birth happens exactly once

The system's own law (the two-seam doctrine): birth is a pure function — the kernel plus your interview answers produce the newborn, deterministically, with no history smuggled in. Everything that happens to a project after its birth commit is an upgrade, and upgrades work completely differently — they are governed by the Kernel Migration Procedure, the standard on the other seam (ask your instance to walk you through it when a crossing comes up).

The interview — six real questions

Started with /begin (or the word begin) in a FRACTAL clone. One question at a time, never a wall of form fields; after every answer the session drafts what your answer becomes and reads it back. Everything not asked takes a sensible default — silence is a legitimate answer, and the receipt (below) records which was which.
  • The project — what are you working on, and why does it deserve a memory? Your words become the newborn's vision, kept verbatim wherever they carry the meaning.
  • The name — what it's called; the tool derives the identity prefix and shows it before committing.
  • The place — where on your machine should it live? Asked, never assumed: nothing is created until you've chosen, and the tool creates exactly that folder. The clone you're sitting in is the hatch — disposable scaffolding; the place you name is the home, the one choice worth making consciously.
  • The writers — your name and real email; a name of your choosing for the AI collaborator (its own identity, not the vendor's).
  • The territories — 2–4 domains in your own vocabulary. Root names are forever, but everything under a root moves freely later — you're naming territories, not committing to a taxonomy.
  • Durability — where the off-site copy lives. An empty repo on your host is the shape; birth without a remote is a fine answer too.
  • On clients with real UI (your ruling, 2026-08-23): the choice-shaped questions render as native panels — structured options you tap, with a free-text door always open — still one at a time. On plainer clients the same questions arrive as chat. Same substance everywhere; the panel is comfort, never a dependency.

What comes into being

  • One folder at the place you chose — the child's entire footprint. Inside: its own governance spine, its own store, its own User Documents/ (born already built, empty, with operating instructions), its own interface place.
  • Born connected: the newborn's first connection record (CN-0001) is its mother — it knows where it came from and can receive mail from day one. It is connected to its mother and to no one else.
  • The day-one receipt — the birth record: which parameters you set, which defaulted on silence, what editions the birth pinned. One minute to read, never changes, and it's what any future audit measures against.
  • The care moment — the one honest breath at the handoff: this is a living system whose integrity is carried by its own small rituals (the checkers, the close, the capture door). Keeping them running is the care it asks — like a pet: it needs feeding, not worship, and drift between feedings is normal because the rituals catch it.
  • After the handoff, the midwife clone governs nothing. Work happens in the child, in its own sessions, under its own authority — and the hatch can be deleted whenever.
